Recurring Play

Recurring Play is a new way to subscribe to National Lottery draw games in the app, so players never miss a draw. As the sole designer, I'm shaping the end-to-end experience: setting up a subscription, paying for it with Apple Pay or a debit card, and managing, pausing or cancelling it later.

My role: UX/UI design, prototyping, AI-assisted design reviews

The Problem

Many players already play every week, but they buy each ticket by hand, and when life gets busy they forget. The existing subscription doesn't fit how they play today. It's web only, covers a handful of games, takes weeks to start, and is hard to change or cancel. Recurring Play brings subscriptions into the app, where most regular players now play, and covers every draw game.

Today

  • Web only, by Direct Debit
  • A few games, and weeks before the first ticket
  • A missed payment cancels the subscription

Recurring Play

  • In the app, paid by Apple Pay or debit card
  • Every draw game, starting from the next draw
  • Weekly, two-weekly or four-weekly plans you can pause or cancel

What players told us

Early research with players showed the idea solved a real problem rather than asking for new behaviour. They described it as “easier”, “simpler” and “automatic”, and one summed up the problem: “life gets in the way”.

Never missing a draw

Especially for players who use the same numbers every week and worry about the draw their numbers come up in.

Predictable spend

Players liked knowing what they'd pay, broken into smaller, manageable amounts.

Staying in control

Convenience only appealed if they could change or stop it easily. So the message isn't “subscribe to the lottery”, it's “never miss a draw”.

Convenience, with control

That became the principle behind every screen. Recurring Play has to be quick to set up, but players must always know exactly what they're signing up to, and how to get out of it.

Set it up once

Subscription Play sits inside the normal play flow as a second way to buy, so regular players don't have to learn a separate journey.

Know what you'll pay

The payment plan shows each option with its price, explains that it's separate from the days you play, and says when the first payment will be taken.

Stop anytime

Every subscription can be edited, paused or cancelled from My Account, and tickets already paid for still play after cancelling.

Setting it up

After choosing their numbers, players pick their draw days and how they want to play: a one-off purchase or Subscription Play. Choosing a subscription adds two short steps, a payment plan and a payment method, each on its own screen with the running total and the game closing time always in view.

Paying and confirming

Before paying, players double-check everything on one summary: their numbers, draw days, payment plan and payment method. With Apple Pay, the payment sheet shows it as a recurring payment, so the commitment is clear at the moment of paying. The confirmation screen then links straight to the new subscription.

Staying in control

Subscriptions live in My Account, grouped as active, paused and cancelled. Each one shows its numbers, next payment, plan and payment method, with clear actions to edit preferences, pause, change the payment method or cancel. Cancelling asks for confirmation once and nothing more, because making it hard to leave would break the trust the whole proposition depends on.

Designing with AI

I used AI inside Figma as a design reviewer. I asked it to critique my screens for hierarchy, copy and accessibility and leave its feedback as annotations, without changing the design, so every decision stayed mine. Giving it context mattered. Reviewing the selector on its own, it flagged missing prices and next steps. Once it saw the full screen, it dropped those and focused on the real issue: the hierarchy between how you buy and how often you pay.

What it found

Frequency, price and the first payment date were spread out, and the subscription settings didn't clearly belong to the Subscription Play option.

What I explored

Two directions that bring them together: accordion cards that expand to show the plan, and a mode selector that switches between one-off and subscription.

What changed

Prices sit next to each plan, the copy became “Choose how often you pay”, the total says it's weekly, and the first payment date sits with the subscription.

Where it stands

Recurring Play is in design. The next step is testing the two set-up directions with players, alongside the edge cases that matter most, like expired cards, failed payments and duplicate subscriptions.
